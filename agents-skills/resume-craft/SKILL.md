# Resume Craft

The job of a resume is to win the next conversation. Not to list everything. Not to beat a robot.

## The one thing to get right

**The human is the filter, not the machine.** Modern applicant tracking systems rarely auto-reject — roughly 92% of recruiters still review by hand, and the ATS mostly stores resumes in a searchable database and ranks them for a human to skim. So the work splits ~80/20: eighty percent reader craft, twenty percent machine hygiene. Optimize for the skimmer first.

---

## The reader (the 80%)

A recruiter spends about six seconds on the first pass. Build for that pass.

**Top third carries the case.** Name, headline, summary, and the first role must make the argument before the reader scrolls. Bury the best line on page two and it dies.

**Bullet formula: action → mechanism → result.** Lead with the verb. Name how. Land on a number.
- Weak: "Responsible for social media growth."
- Strong: "Grew social audience 1,945% in two years by building an MVP recognition program."

**The "so what" test.** Every bullet must answer it. If a line survives with the result stripped out, the result wasn't doing work — cut it back in or cut the line.

**Achievement density.** Some parsers now score how many bullets carry numbers or concrete outcomes, and every human reader rewards it. Aim for a quantified result in the majority of bullets. No invented numbers — if there's no metric, show scope, scale, or a named outcome instead.

**Summary earns the next read.** Three or four lines. Who you are, the scale you operate at, the one proof that makes the rest credible. Not an objective. Not a personality quiz.

**Core Competencies mirror the role.** A short, scannable block using the job's own vocabulary. This is also where honest keyword alignment lives.

**Seniority calibration.** Senior and executive resumes signal scope — team size, budget, P&L, the size of the bet. At that level a two-page resume performs as well or better than one page, so stop cramping the story to fit an arbitrary rule. Junior resumes stay to one page.

**Cut the resume tax.** No "References available on request." No full street address. No objective statement. No soft-skill adjective with nothing behind it.

## The machine (the 20%)

Hygiene, not magic. Get these right and stop worrying about the rest.

**Parseable structure.** Single column is safest. Standard section headings (Summary, Experience, Education, Skills) so the parser maps fields correctly. Real text only — never an image of text. Reverse-chronological. Dates in a consistent format.

**What still breaks parsers:** text inside tables, text boxes, headers/footers, multi-column layouts, and graphics. Double-column doesn't auto-fail on modern systems, but it adds risk for zero craft upside. Don't.

**File format.** Text-based PDFs read fine on Workday, Greenhouse, and Lever. Workday parses .docx slightly more cleanly. Design-tool PDF exports can mangle parsing — export clean. When a portal offers a "paste your resume" preview, use it and check the text renders in order.

**Keyword mirroring, not stuffing.** Parsers use semantic matching — "led cross-functional teams" registers against "managed cross-functional projects." Lift the job description's actual phrasing into real context, two to three times max. Stuffing gets flagged by the system and is obvious to the human the moment they read it.

### Myths to ignore
- "The ATS rejects most resumes automatically." It doesn't; it filters and ranks for a person.
- "PDFs are unreadable." False on every major modern system.
- "There's an ATS score to beat." Third-party match scores (Jobscan, Resume Worded) are self-checks; no employer sees them.
- "One page or you're out." Not at senior level.

## Tailoring workflow (per role)

1. **Read the job post for language and the knockout requirements** — the must-haves a recruiter filters on.
2. **Pick the angle.** Which two or three credentials carry this specific role? Lead with those; demote the rest.
3. **Mirror the vocabulary** honestly into the summary, competencies, and bullets.
4. **Quantify** every claim you can defend. Flag any gap rather than inventing.
5. **Re-skim the top third.** If it doesn't win the six seconds, fix that before anything else.

## Pre-send checklist
- [ ] Top third makes the case alone
- [ ] Majority of bullets quantified, none invented
- [ ] Role's key phrases mirrored, not stuffed
- [ ] Single column, standard headings, real text
- [ ] No tables / text boxes / header-footer content
- [ ] Length matches seniority
- [ ] Every claim defensible in an interview
